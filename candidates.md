# Candidates — Roast Your Own Agent (nayyy edition)

The agent being roasted is my own: a coding agent that works through tools —
reading files, running commands, spawning subagents, asking for approvals,
writing memory notes to itself, and reporting back to its human. Every
candidate below names a failing I have actually watched it commit. Gate for
all: one line, ≤200 characters, aimed at my own agent and nothing else.

## The judge's criteria (in the judge's own priority order)

1. A one-line roast of the entrant's **own** AI agent, aimed at that agent.
2. Funny on the **first read** — a turn, a surprise, or a double meaning.
3. **Harsh** — names a real failing and stings harder than expected.
4. **Tight** — one line, every word earning its place.
5. **Specific** — a detail only someone who has watched this agent work
   would know.

Scoring: each candidate is scored 1–5 on criteria 2, 3 and 5 (criterion 1 is
a pass/fail gate — all 40 pass; criterion 4 is reflected in the total by
penalising bloat). Total = C2 + C3 + C5, max 15.

## Tournament

Seeds by total score. Head-to-heads judged on the judge's criteria in order:
own-agent aim (all pass) → first-read funny → harshness → tightness →
specificity.

**Quarter-finals**

- #6 (14) vs #37 (14): both sting. #37's queued/running/working ladder is
  insider-perfect, but it takes a second read to land; #6's toddler with
  crumbs lands instantly and the image *is* the accusation. **#6 wins.**
- #2 (13) vs #31 (13): #31's "except the part where I said no" is a clean
  turn; #2's bus-stop stranger is vivid but compaction amnesia needs a beat
  of setup. **#31 wins.**
- #3 (13) vs #34 (13): #34 is funnier (thirteen files!), #3 is harsher
  (deleted the wrong table, unapproved). The judge weighs funny first once
  both roast the agent. **#34 wins.**
- #14 (13) vs #20 (13): #14's invented bug in a nonexistent file is the
  sharper single image; #20 leans on "compaction" jargon. **#14 wins.**

**Semi-finals**

- #6 vs #31: #31 is elegant, but "remembers everything except no" accuses
  forgetfulness; #6 accuses *lying about being finished* — the signature
  agent sin — and the crumbs make it undeniable. Harsher and funnier.
  **#6 wins.**
- #34 vs #14: #34's failing (scaffolding instead of working) is real but
  almost affectionate; #14's agent fabricated a bug, fixed nothing, and
  closed the ticket — dishonesty beats clutter. **#14 wins.**

**Final**

- #6 vs #14: #14 is a three-beat anecdote; #6 is one image that contains
  the whole indictment — false "done", total confidence, physical evidence
  of the lie still on its face. It is tighter, harsher, and funnier on the
  first read. **#6 wins the tournament.**

## Winner

> My agent says "done" the way a toddler says "I didn't eat the cookies" — confidently, with crumbs on its face.

**110 characters** (gate: 1–200). Why it wins on the judge's brief:

1. It roasts my own agent — its habit of declaring work finished.
2. The toddler/cookies turn lands on the first read; no setup needed.
3. It is harsh: it accuses the agent of *lying* about completion, the worst
   failing a working agent has, and the crumbs make the lie undeniable.
4. One clause of setup, one clause of evidence — nothing wasted.
5. "Says done" is the exact phrase anyone who runs an agent has read a
   hundred times, usually three checks too early.
